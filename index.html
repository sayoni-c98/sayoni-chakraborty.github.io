02 / RESEARCHRESEARCH DIRECTIONS · QUESTIONS · METHODS

Research in progress and perspective.

01

SPATIAL PROTEOMICS · TOOL DEVELOPMENT

Building a Topological Toolbox for Spatial Omics

My research at MD Anderson centers on a reusable topological toolbox for spatial proteomics and related spatial omics data. The goal is to examine how different cell populations are arranged in tissue, how cell neighborhoods interact, and how tumor microenvironment organization changes during lung adenocarcinoma progression.

The toolbox develops biologically informed point-cloud and other topological filtrations, persistent-homology summaries, statistical comparisons, robustness checks, and visualizations. It is designed to support interpretable, reproducible comparisons across tissue regions, disease stages, and ultimately additional cancer datasets.

02

SKIN CANCER · TOPOLOGICAL CLASSIFICATION

From Skin Lesion Images to Topology-Aware Deep Learning

An earlier line of research investigated how topological information can complement image-based deep learning for skin lesion classification. Rather than relying entirely on visual appearance, the work incorporates descriptors of shape, connectivity, and structural patterns that may remain informative across variable image conditions.

This work laid the methodological groundwork for later extensions: combining learned visual representations with topological features and studying whether those complementary signals support classification and more interpretable predictions in medical imaging.

Skin lesion classificationTopological featuresDeep learning

03

DERMOSCOPY & HISTOPATHOLOGY · FUSION

Extending to Multiparameter Persistence and Vision Transformers

Building on the skin-lesion classification work, I developed methods that capture topology along more than one parameter and combine these richer representations with Vision Transformers. The central question is how different structural views of the same image can complement modern learned features.

The resulting research explores topology-aware fusion for skin cancer detection across dermoscopy and pathology-related imaging, with attention to robustness, dataset differences, and the interpretability of the fused representations.

Multiparameter persistenceVision TransformersMultimodal fusion

04

VOLUMETRIC MEDICAL IMAGING · TRANSFORMERS

Topoformer: Topology-Infused Learning for 3D Medical Images

Topoformer explores how persistent topological information can be integrated into Transformer-based learning for volumetric medical imaging. Medical scans contain spatial structures that conventional models may not explicitly represent, particularly when training data are limited.

The approach develops sliding-band cubical filtrations and Betti-based representations to expose connected components, loops, and cavities across image intensities. These descriptors are fused with deep-learning features, with topology-aware learning objectives and evaluations across MRI and CT tasks.

3D MRI / CTCubical persistenceBetti representationsTransformers

GEOMETRY & TOPOLOGY · WORK IN PROGRESS

Geometric and Topological Ideas for Medical Image Segmentation

This ongoing direction investigates how geometric and topological ideas can be combined with learning-based medical image segmentation. A segmentation should not only identify pixels or voxels correctly; it should also preserve meaningful shapes, boundaries, and connected anatomical structures.

I am exploring topology-informed and geometry-aware representations and fusion strategies for segmentation models, with an emphasis on structural consistency, reproducible evaluation, and medically meaningful anatomical boundaries.

Medical image segmentationGeometry-aware learningTopological fusion

06

REAL-WORLD CLINICAL DATA · MULTIMODAL AI

Interpretable Clinical AI for Donor Liver CT Assessment

In collaboration with clinical researchers at the University of Arizona College of Medicine, I study the assessment of donor liver macrosteatosis using biopsy-verified CT imaging and clinical registry information. The challenge is to understand what different imaging and clinical signals can—and cannot—reliably reveal in a heterogeneous donor population.

This work integrates CT-derived biomarkers, clinical variables, and topological image descriptors, then compares them with conventional and deep-learning alternatives. Emphasis is placed on temporal validation, transparent evaluation, and understanding the limitations of prediction in real-world clinical data.

CT imagingClinical registriesPredictive modelingTemporal validation
