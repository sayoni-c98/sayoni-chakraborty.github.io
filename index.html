02 / RESEARCHRESEARCH DIRECTIONS · METHODS · BIOMEDICAL APPLICATIONS

Research

My research lies at the intersection of topological data analysis, machine learning, deep learning, and biomedical data science. I develop mathematically informed and interpretable learning methods for high-dimensional biomedical data, with applications in medical imaging, cancer research, spatial omics, and clinical prediction.

A central focus of my work is integrating topological and geometric representations with modern machine learning architectures to capture structural information that conventional approaches may overlook. My research spans persistent homology, multimodal feature fusion, CNNs, Vision Transformers, contrastive representation learning, and statistical modeling, with an emphasis on robust evaluation, interpretability, and real-world biomedical applications.

01

SPATIAL OMICS · CANCER RESEARCH

Topological Learning for Spatial Omics and Cancer Research

My current research focuses on developing topological and graph-based learning methods to investigate the spatial organization of cells within complex biological tissues, particularly in cancer. I am interested in how cellular heterogeneity, spatial interactions, and higher-order structural patterns within the tumor microenvironment relate to cancer development and progression.

At MD Anderson Cancer Center, I am developing a reusable topological toolbox for spatial omics analysis that integrates point-cloud representations, cell-neighborhood graphs, biologically informed filtrations, persistent homology, topology-derived descriptors, and statistical analysis. Applied to high-dimensional spatial proteomics data from lung adenocarcinoma, this work aims to characterize cellular connectivity, tissue architecture, and spatial relationships across disease stages. The broader goal is to develop interpretable, robust, and reproducible approaches for identifying biologically meaningful patterns in complex cancer datasets.

02

3D MEDICAL IMAGING · DEEP LEARNING

Topology-Infused Transformers for 3D Medical Image Analysis

My research investigates how topological representations can be integrated into deep learning architectures for analyzing high-dimensional volumetric medical images. Three-dimensional MRI and CT contain complex anatomical structures that conventional deep learning models may not explicitly capture, particularly when training data are limited.

I developed Topoformer, a topology-informed Transformer framework that incorporates sliding-band cubical filtrations and Betti-based representations into volumetric image learning. The framework integrates multiscale topological descriptors with learned imaging features and topology-aware contrastive learning to capture complementary structural information. Applications include brain MRI tumor classification and 3D medical image analysis, with an emphasis on data-efficient learning, model robustness, computational efficiency, and interpretable representations.

03

MULTIPARAMETER TOPOLOGY · FEATURE FUSION

Multiparameter Persistent Homology and Contrastive Multimodal Learning

A major direction of my research involves developing hybrid deep learning frameworks that combine multiparameter persistent homology, contrastive representation learning, and multimodal feature fusion. Multiparameter persistence captures structural information across interacting filtration parameters, providing richer topological descriptors and representations of complex biomedical images.

I develop approaches that extract multiparameter topological features from dermoscopic images and integrate them with Vision Transformers through supervised contrastive learning. By fusing topology-derived descriptors with learned visual representations, this research investigates complementary structural information for skin cancer classification. The work emphasizes robust feature fusion, cross-dataset generalization, interpretable representations, and understanding the contribution of topological information to model predictions.

04

PERSISTENT HOMOLOGY · CANCER IMAGING

Persistent Homology and Hybrid Deep Learning for Cancer Image Classification

My research explores how persistent homology and topology-based feature extraction can reveal structural and morphological patterns in biomedical images. I develop topological descriptors using cubical filtrations, persistence diagrams, Betti curves, and vectorized persistence representations to characterize connectivity, shape, spatial organization, and multiscale patterns across image intensities.

These topology-derived features are integrated with machine learning and deep learning architectures, including CNNs and hybrid models, to complement conventional image representations. Applications include dermoscopic skin lesion classification and ovarian and breast cancer histopathology analysis. This research emphasizes interpretable feature representations, complementary structural information, and robust classification of complex biomedical imaging data.

Cubical persistencePersistence diagramsHybrid CNN modelsCancer imaging

05

CLINICAL PREDICTION · MULTIMODAL MACHINE LEARNING

Multimodal Clinical AI for Organ Transplantation and Predictive Modeling

My research also focuses on developing multimodal machine learning models for real-world clinical datasets, integrating medical imaging, patient-level clinical information, and topology-derived features for clinically relevant prediction tasks. I am particularly interested in high-dimensional data integration, predictive modeling, class imbalance, interpretable feature representations, and model generalization.

In collaboration with clinical researchers at the University of Arizona College of Medicine, I developed machine learning pipelines for donor liver macrosteatosis assessment using biopsy-verified CT imaging and clinical registry data from 2,709 deceased donors. This work integrates CT-derived imaging biomarkers, topological descriptors, and donor clinical variables into multimodal prediction models. My contributions include clinical data processing, topology-based feature extraction, feature engineering, model development, statistical evaluation, benchmarking, and temporal validation. The broader goal is to investigate how complementary imaging and clinical features can support organ assessment and transplantation research.

06

GEOMETRY & TOPOLOGY · MEDICAL SEGMENTATION

Geometry- and Topology-Guided Medical Image Segmentation

I am investigating how geometric and topological representations can complement deep learning approaches for medical image segmentation. Conventional segmentation models primarily learn pixel- or voxel-level features, while anatomical structures also exhibit important topological and geometric properties, including connectivity, boundaries, shape, and spatial relationships.

This ongoing research explores integrating topology-derived descriptors and geometry-aware representations with deep learning segmentation architectures. I am particularly interested in topology-informed feature fusion and learning strategies that can preserve meaningful anatomical structures, improve structural consistency, and support robust segmentation in complex, high-dimensional medical imaging data.
